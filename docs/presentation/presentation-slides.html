Business Problem Statement

The Real-World Problem

Pratham's ASER survey collects millions of handwritten Hindi responses from 600,000+ households across rural India. Today, every response must be manually transcribed before analysis — a massive bottleneck that delays educational interventions.

The Bottleneck

  • Manual transcription: ~50 pages/person/day
  • Millions of responses per survey cycle
  • Teachers manage 60+ students — no time for individual feedback
  • Delays in transcription = delays in intervention

Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Traditional OCR

  • Tesseract — poor on Devanagari ligatures
  • Google Vision — fails on low-resource handwritten Hindi
  • Amazon Textract — English-centric, no Indic support

LLMs

  • Opus-class models: partial capability but cost-prohibitive at scale
  • Cheap LLMs (Haiku): 0% accuracy on handwritten Hindi

Our Solution

  • Fine-tuned TrOCR on domain-specific Hindi handwriting
  • YOLOv8 for language-agnostic word detection
  • Hindi RoBERTa decoder for vocabulary coverage
  • Decoupled pipeline — each stage independently improvable
  • Outputs clean Unicode text for any downstream system
06 / 17
Technical Problem Statement

Why Handwritten Hindi is Technically Hard

Complex Script Structure

Devanagari matras attach above, below, and around base consonants; conjuncts fuse multiple consonants. Character boundaries are ambiguous.

No Clear Word Boundaries

A shared shirorekha (top bar) runs across letters; matras extend vertically. No reliable whitespace signal unlike English.

High Writer Variability

Same word looks completely different across writers. CER ranged from 21% (clearest) to 100% (most erratic) in our 11-group evaluation.

Matras attach above, below, and around base consonants

Conjuncts fuse multiple consonants

Source

IIIT-Hyderabad Indic Handwriting Dataset - a publicly available research corpus. Each image shows exactly one handwritten Hindi word, pre-cropped and isolated, written by one of hundreds of different writers. Labels were created by human annotators (the ground-truth text for each crop).

Dataset Coverage

The 95,430 images cover 11,029 distinct Hindi words, with roughly 8–9 different writers each writing the same word. The model learns each word across many handwriting styles — key to generalising across writers.

The words are Hindi vocabulary items sampled from real written text, covering a broad range of characters, conjuncts, and matras.

Role in our pipeline

Used exclusively to fine-tune and evaluate TrOCR (the recognition model). The 8.17% CER result comes from the 12,869-image test split of this dataset — perfect crops, no detector involved.

Used to train YOLOv8 to localise words on real survey pages - the same domain as our end-to-end evaluation.

How We Measure

CER counts wrong characters. Exact word match requires the full word to be correct. A 50% CER does not mean 50% words wrong — exact match is only 13.4%.

v2 — What Didn't Work (honest)
  • LM rescoring on field — HindiSeg-skewed char 3-gram added ~0.6pp CER; disabled by default for Pratham scans (LM_ENABLE_FIELD=false).
  • Inter-annotator κ = 0.62 — target 0.8; dual labels only 180 / 881 portal rows; gold set ~140 rows vs 400 hoped.
  • Field word-exact ~12% — not safe for unattended grading; HITL mandatory on field uploads in v2.
  • YOLO merge heuristic — ~3% of dense rows over-merge adjacent words.
  • HindiSeg TrOCR gain modest — 8.17% → 7.12% vs ~5% aspiration (checkpoint saturation).
  • Monitoring SLOs — pipeline/monitoring/ is scaffolding; thresholds not calibrated on live ASER traffic.
  • HITL throughput ~3× manual — below 8× stretch (review UX latency).
